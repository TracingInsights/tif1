# E8: a performance regression gate in CI

## 1. Hypothesis

Each round's wins were only protected by the next round's baseline. The Delphi
harness needs a primed 290 MB cache that CI does not have, and the
`benchmarks` job is not a gate. A small vendored fixture plus a pytest gate can
catch both kinds of regression on every PR, offline and in under 2 minutes:
in-process phase times of the fresh-process warm workloads, compared with a
committed baseline, and the user-visible surfaces, compared with a committed
parity digest. The gate must pass on the control tree and fail on a deliberate
slowdown (`time.sleep(0.05)` in `Session.laps`).

## 2. What changed

No library code changed (`src/` is identical to b5b91ed).

- `tools/perf/perf_gate_fixture.py` (new) builds
  `tests/fixtures/perf/payloads.bin.xz` (6.96 MB) from any tif1 cache directory
  that holds the sessions (`--source-cache`), or fetches them first (`--fetch`).
  The fixture is a payload bundle, not a SQLite file. It holds an xz-compressed
  manifest and the raw payload bytes: the 13 JSON payloads of the 2025 Bahrain
  race and qualifying (laps of all 20 drivers, drivers, weather, race control,
  session info, the Stats classification), plus 105 telemetry laps (race VER;
  qualifying PIA, the pole lap, VER and LEC). The other 1300 telemetry keys are
  listed as "missing". The gate seeds a new cache **through the `Cache` API of
  the tree under test** (`set_raw`, `set_telemetry`, `record_missing`). A
  committed SQLite file would go stale on the next frame-tier
  `_FRAME_TIER_SCHEMA_VERSION` bump or table-layout change; this fixture does
  not. The missing verdicts make `fetch_all_laps_telemetry()` skip the other
  drivers through the existing CDN-404 path. Without them it would make one
  failing offline fetch per lap.
- `tools/perf/perf_gate.py` (new) is the engine and CLI:
  - `--seed`: the seeding child.
  - `--child <workload>`: the timed child. It is adapted from
    `delphi_warm_bench._child`. There are 10 workloads: `import_get_session`,
    `laps`, `results`, `state`, `driver_tel`, `car_data_loop`, `fastest_lap_tel`,
    `quali_split`, `all_tel` (qualifying) and `notebook` (qualifying, on the
    three telemetry drivers).
  - `--digest <lib>`: the parity digest. It is adapted from `delphi_parity.py`
    and covers 89 surfaces per backend.
  - `run_gate`, `compare`, `format_table` and `write_outputs` do the
    orchestration and comparison.

  The children get a clean environment: no inherited `TIF1_*`/`PYTEST_*`
  variables, `TIF1_OFFLINE_MODE=true`, and a temporary `HOME`, so a developer's
  `~/.tif1rc` cannot change what is measured.

  The comparison works as follows:
  - **Machine factor.** This is the median of best/baseline-best over every
    phase ≥ 2 ms, clamped to [1/3, 3]. CI runners are not this box, and one slow
    phase cannot move a median of ~120 phases.
  - **Baseline.** It stores the **min and median of 7 runs** per phase. A
    phase's best of 3 fails when it is above `median × factor × 1.5` and more
    than 5 ms above `median × factor`. Steady phases (median ≈ min) keep a tight
    limit. Phases with a wide spread get the room that spread needs. Polars
    `fastest_lap_tel`, where numpy first loads, ranges from 67 to 145 ms over 15
    runs, and `driver_fastest_tel` from 11.5 to 28.3 ms.
  - **Confirmation.** A workload with a flagged phase is run 5 more times, and
    the best time is kept. A descheduled run is noise; a regression is slow on
    every run.
  - **Digests.** Each backend gets two digests: a cold one (the first pass,
    which builds frames from the payload tier and writes the frame tiers) and a
    warm one (after the timed runs, read back from the frame tiers).
- `tests/perf_gate/` (new, marker `perf_gate`) is the pytest wrapper with 6
  tests:
  - `test_phases_within_baseline` prints the full table and fails with a
    table of the regressed phases.
  - `test_parity_digest[pandas|polars-cold|warm]`.
  - `test_gate_flags_injected_slowdown`: a self-test. With
    `TIF1_PERF_GATE_INJECT_SLEEP=0.05`, the child wraps `Session.laps` with a
    50 ms sleep. The `laps` phases must be flagged when compared with the clean
    run's machine factor.

  `--write-baseline` (a pytest option, or the CLI flag) rewrites the baseline
  and digests. It merges the measured workloads into the baseline, so a
  `--libs`/`--workloads` subset keeps the rest.
- `tests/fixtures/perf/` (new): the bundle, `baseline.json` (11 KB, min and median
  per phase), `parity_pandas.json` (304 KB) and `parity_polars.json` (311 KB).
  The digest files have one surface per line, so a changed surface is a
  one-line diff. Two delta files hold what the cold pass does differently:
  `parity_pandas_cold_delta.json` (60 KB, one surface, see §6) and
  `parity_polars_cold_delta.json` (empty).
- `tests/unit/test_perf_gate_compare.py` (new, 15 tests, offline, < 0.5 s)
  covers the gate's pure logic on every unit run: the factor and its clamp, the
  median-based limit, the absolute floor, the tolerance, missing and new
  phases, the digest diff (columns, list members, nested `all_tel` frames,
  added and removed surfaces), the bundle round trip, the `write_outputs` round
  trip (baseline merge, warm + cold delta), the clean child environment, and
  consistency of the committed files (bundle < 10 MB).
- `.github/workflows/ci.yml`:
  - A new `perf-gate` job: Linux, Python 3.13 (the interpreter the
    baseline and digests are written with), `uv sync --all-extras`, then
    `pytest -o addopts='' -p no:xdist tests/perf_gate -m perf_gate -v -s`, with a
    15-minute timeout.
  - The `test` job's marker expression gains `and not perf_gate`.
- `pyproject.toml`: registers the `perf_gate` marker. The default addopts
  become `-m "not benchmark and not perf_gate"`, so `pytest tests/` deselects
  the gate (verified: "6 deselected").
- `prek.toml`: `check-added-large-files` excludes the bundle. Without this, a
  local commit with the hook installed would reject the 7 MB file.
- `AGENTS.md` (build/test section and the tests layout line) and
  `CONTRIBUTING.md`: how to run the gate and when to rewrite the baseline.

## 3. Measurements

This hypothesis adds no library change, so there is no control-vs-candidate
A/B. The measurements are those the section asks for: the job's runtime, a pass
on the control tree, and a fail on a deliberate slowdown. They also cover the
false-positive rate, which decides whether a gate is usable.

### Runtime of the gate (this box, 32 cores, other agents running)

| run | wall |
|---|---|
| gate check on the clean tree (pytest, 6 tests) | **46.7 s** (setup 43.5 s: seed ~1 s, 2 cold digests, 2 × 10 workloads × 3 reps, 2 warm digests; self-test 3.0 s) |
| `--write-baseline` (7 reps) | 75-81 s |
| check with a real regression (17 workloads re-measured 5×) | 98 s |

A GitHub `ubuntu-latest` runner is maybe 1.5-2x slower, so expect about 70-95 s
of tests plus `uv sync`. That is inside the 2-minute target for the tests and
the 15-minute job timeout.

### Pass on the control tree (`src/` == b5b91ed)

Clean pytest run: machine factor 1.05, **0 of 120 phases regressed**, all 4
digests at 0 differing surfaces, and the self-test passed.

False-positive check: 6 consecutive full gate runs (`run_gate` with the
baseline and confirmation, timings only), while other agents were loading the
box:

| run | factor | regressed | re-measured | worst phase (best / limit, ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1.005 | 0 | - | polars fastest_lap_tel/driver_fastest_tel 12.1 / 14.4 |
| 1 | 1.015 | 0 | polars/fastest_lap_tel | polars fastest_lap_tel/driver_fastest_tel 12.2 / 14.5 |
| 2 | 1.024 | 0 | - | polars fastest_lap_tel/driver_fastest_tel 13.6 / 14.6 |
| 3 | 1.016 | 0 | polars/fastest_lap_tel | polars notebook/load 270.4 / 349.0 |
| 4 | 1.018 | 0 | - | polars fastest_lap_tel/driver_fastest_tel 12.8 / 14.6 |
| 5 | 1.026 | 0 | - | polars fastest_lap_tel/driver_fastest_tel 12.1 / 14.6 |

**0 false positives in 6 runs.** The confirmation step was needed twice, and it
cleared the suspect both times. These 6 runs ran against an interim baseline
that held only mins (the median-based limit had a merge bug in
`write_outputs`, since fixed and unit-tested). That makes their limits
*stricter* than the committed min/median baseline, so the result is
conservative.

Before the confirmation step and the median-based limits existed, the first
naive version (min of 3 against the min of 3, 5 ms floor) produced false
positives in 2 of 2 runs:
- polars `driver_fastest_tel` 16.7 vs 15.0 and 20.2 vs 15.1;
- pandas `get_session` 570 vs 515 (a spike in the pandas import under box
  load).

### Fail on a deliberate slowdown

1. **Self-test (in the job).** A 50 ms sleep is injected into `Session.laps`
   by the child (pandas, `laps` + `results`, 1 rep). Both `laps` phases are
   flagged (about 53.5 ms against a ~8.5 ms limit). The test passes.
2. **Real library edit, as the section specifies (temporary, reverted, not
   committed).** I added `time.sleep(0.05)` at the top of `Session.laps` in
   `src/tif1/core.py`, behind an env var, and ran
   `python tools/perf/perf_gate.py` with it on. The result was **exit 1, 21 of
   120 phases regressed**, with machine factor 1.05 (unaffected):
   - The `laps` phase of every workload on both backends, about 53.5 ms against
     8.5-11 ms limits, ratio 9-19x.
   - Phases that call `Session.laps` internally: pandas `driver_fastest_tel`
     78.5/44.6, `split` 61.8/17.9, notebook `load` 316/264 and `fastest_lap_tel`;
     polars `split` 144/96 and notebook `fastest_lap_tel` 51/6.7.

   The parity digests stayed at 0 differences, as expected for a timing-only
   change. The gate run took 98 s.

### Parity

The gate's own digests: 0 differing surfaces, pandas and polars, cold and warm
(89 surfaces each), on the control tree. The Elis control digests
(`/tmp/elis_parity_*.json`) are not affected: `src/` is unchanged, so this
hypothesis has no parity surface.

### Unit tests, ruff, ty

- `pytest tests/unit -n 6`: **3427 passed** (the new 15 included).
- `ruff check src/ tests/ tools/perf/perf_gate*.py`: clean. `ruff format`:

- `ty check src/tif1`: clean.

## 4. Verdict

**SHIP.** The gate passes on the control tree: 0/120 phases in the pytest run
and 0 false positives in 6 repeated runs under load. It fails on the
deliberate 50 ms `Session.laps` slowdown, both through the in-job self-test and
through a real edit of `core.py` (21 phases flagged on both backends). It runs
in ~47 s here, it is offline, and it needs no SQLite fixture that a schema
change could invalidate. On its first run, the parity half already found a
real cold/warm inconsistency (§6).

## 5. CHANGELOG paragraph

- CI has a performance regression gate (`perf-gate` job,
  `tests/perf_gate`, marker `perf_gate`). It seeds a cache from a 7 MB vendored
  payload bundle: the 2025 Bahrain race and qualifying, with the telemetry of
  four drivers. It then times 10 fresh-process warm workloads on both backends,
  offline. A phase fails when its best of 3 is above 1.5x (and more than 5 ms
  above) the baseline median, after scaling by the runner's speed (the median
  ratio over all ~120 phases) and 5 confirmation reruns. A parity digest of 89
  surfaces per backend, on the cold build path and on the warm frame-tier path,
  must match the committed one. The job takes about 47 s of tests on a 32-core
  machine. It passes on the current tree, and it fails when `Session.laps`
  sleeps for 50 ms. `--write-baseline` rewrites the baseline and digests when
  a change is meant to move them.

## 6. Notes for the integrator

- **Pre-existing inconsistency found by the gate (not fixed here, frozen in
  `parity_pandas_cold_delta.json`).** On pandas, `fetch_all_laps_telemetry()`
  returns plain `pd.DataFrame` values when it builds the frames from payloads:
  cold cache, first load, or a load that is only partly in the frame tier. It
  returns `Telemetry` values when it reads them from the frame tier. Values,
  dtypes and index are identical; only the class differs. The cause:
  `Session._frames_from_payloads` and the network branch of
  `fetch_all_laps_telemetry_async` memoize the result of `_create_telemetry_df`
  without wrapping it. The frame-tier read passes
  `frame_class=self._telemetry_frame_class()`. So the `Telemetry` methods
  (`add_distance()` and the rest) are on the warm result and not on the cold
  one. I inferred this from the class in the digest; I did not run it. Polars is consistent
  (`_bind_telemetry` wraps it). A fix is a small follow-up: wrap in
  `_bind_telemetry` on pandas as well, or pass through the frame class. That
  fix will change `parity_pandas_cold_delta.json` to `{}`. That is the gate
  working as intended: rewrite with `--write-baseline` and review the one-line
  diff.
- **Rewrite the baseline/digests when:**
  - a PR is meant to move a phase (commit the new `baseline.json` with it);
  - a PR changes a user-visible value;
  - `uv.lock` bumps pandas/polars/numpy/pyarrow, because the digests hash
    `repr`s and dtype names.

  The digests were written with exactly the `uv.lock` versions (verified: 0
  mismatches between the venv and the lock) on Python 3.13, which is why the
  job pins 3.13.
- **Baseline machine.** The committed baseline was written on this 32-core
  box. The machine factor absorbs a uniform speed difference. If the runner's
  *relative* speeds differ a lot (for example SQLite I/O vs CPU), regenerate
  the baseline from a CI run. The job prints the full table (`-s`), and
  `python tools/perf/perf_gate.py --json out.json` writes the measured phases.
  A follow-up could upload that file as an artifact.
- **Known blind spot.** A slowdown of *every* phase by the same ratio reads as
  a slower machine. The factor is printed on the table's first line. Treat a
  factor far from what the runner usually shows (≈1.0 here) as a signal. The
  clamp at 3x bounds the damage. A stronger variant is an in-job A/B against
  the PR base (check out `origin/main` into a worktree and interleave). That
  costs ~2x the runtime and needs no committed baseline. It is worth doing if
  the committed baseline proves fragile on runners.
- **Sensitivity.** Short phases need more than +5 ms (beyond the 1.5x) to fail.
  A 4 ms phase growing to 8 ms passes; a 50 ms sleep is caught everywhere it
  lands. The tolerance and floor are CLI flags (`--tolerance`,
  `--abs-floor-ms`). The pytest wrapper uses the defaults.
- **`all_tel` on the fixture is not the Delphi `all_tel`.** It reads 48 laps
  from the frame tier and skips 229 known-missing laps. The skip costs about
  0.5 ms per missing lap (pandas `all_tel` ~160 ms for 48 laps, against
  Delphi's 89 ms for 1128 laps). This is a real path (CDN 404s), but if it ever
  becomes the dominant cost of the workload, add the qualifying's remaining
  telemetry to the bundle (+1.3 MB per driver) or trim the session.
- The fixture bundle can be regenerated without the network from any cache
  that holds the two sessions:
  `python tools/perf/perf_gate_fixture.py --source-cache ~/.cache/tif1`.
  `--fetch` primes a temporary cache online first. I did not run `--fetch` here
  (the bundle came from the private warm cache, `--source-cache`).
- `git add` of a 7 MB binary: the hypothesis allowed up to ~10 MB. A unit test
  pins it below 10 MB.
