# N7: a smaller columnar telemetry format (`tel.v2`), lossless predicted channels

## 1. Hypothesis

A cold telemetry load is bound by bytes on the wire. Gortyn G3's `tel.v1`
(ten laps per file, typed byte-shuffled columns, one zstd-19 frame) took the
2025 Bahrain race from 71.3 MB of gzipped lap JSON to 52.5 MB and stopped at
the float64 channels whose mantissas look random (~65% of a file). The data
repositories have not published v1, so a better v2 can replace it. N7 asked
whether those channels are decimal values in disguise, exact functions of
other channels, or compressible with better float codecs, and whether a
lossless v2 (decoded laps equal to the native JSON decode bit for bit)
shrinks the files enough to move the cold `all_tel` / `driver_tel` /
notebook paths on the G3 throttled local CDN.

## 2. What changed

The answer was "exact functions of other channels", not a better generic
float codec. Every float channel the files carry is computed upstream by
fastf1 (`Lap.get_telemetry()`) or by the data pipeline from a few raw
channels. The channel study on the warm cache (all 1405 laps) found:

- fastf1's `merge_channels` interpolates over the `Date` index, which pandas
  hands to numpy/scipy as int64 nanoseconds converted to float64. The
  live-timing dates are millisecond-precise, so a row's date is
  `float64(T0 + time_ms * 10**6)` with `T0` the lap's start date, and only
  `T0 mod 512` matters (float64 nanosecond dates of 2006-2043 are multiples
  of 256 and `10**6 ≡ 64 mod 512`). With that offset (searched per lap, 512
  candidates):
  - `numpy.interp`'s formula reproduces `rpm`, `speed`, `throttle` and
    `DistanceToDriverAhead` at the position rows **exactly** (100% of the
    interior rows of the laps probed);
  - a quadratic interpolating spline with scipy's `make_interp_spline(k=2)`
    knots reproduces `distance` at the position rows (scipy itself: 100% of
    interior rows) and `x`/`y`/`z` at the car rows (exact on about two laps in
    three, a few ULPs elsewhere).
- `distance` at the car rows is `prev + speed / 3.6 * dt` (fastf1's
  `integrate_distance`): 99.4% exact.
- `rel_distance = distance / D` per lap (fastf1's `add_relative_distance`):
  99.9% within ±4 ULP.
- `acc_x`, `acc_y`, `acc_z` are the pipeline's acceleration formulas
  (gradients, a hold of jumps that takes the previous *input* sample, a 3- or
  9-sample moving average, `arctan2`/`unwrap` for the lateral and vertical
  ones): ~2-4 bits of residual per sample instead of ~50.

Things that did not help or were not available: the floats are not
decimals (`time` is the only `dec` channel); XOR/linear predictors gain ~1%
(G3); pcodec could not be evaluated (`pip install` is denied in this
sandbox; the native crate builds offline only with its locked crates). ALP,
Chimp and Gorilla target decimal or slowly varying floats; these mantissas
are neither, so they were not pursued once the derivations were found.

Files:

- `src/tif1/core_utils/columnar_telemetry.py`: `tel.v2` codec (replaces v1;
  magic `TIF1TEL\x02`, file `{driver}/tel.v2.{first}.bin`, same container).
  - A predicted channel stores the zigzag-coded ULP distance between each
    sample and a prediction computed with IEEE `+ - * /` (and `fmod`) only,
    never libm or a fused multiply-add; a NaN prediction becomes 0.0. Every
    bit pattern round-trips (NaN payloads, `-0.0`).
  - Header additions: `anchors` (packbits of the car rows: whole `rpm`,
    `speed`, `throttle`) and `dates` (one int64 lap start per lap).
  - Encodings: `interp` (rpm/speed/throttle/DTDA at the position rows),
    `lerp` (fallback without dates: the interpolation fraction `rpm` shows),
    `spline` (x/y/z at the car rows: whole samples as in `split`, the rest as
    residuals against the lap's spline), `integ`/`integs` (distance: the
    integration chain at the car rows, the spline at the position rows),
    `ratio` (rel_distance), `accx` (acc_x), `lat` (acc_y/acc_z, with an
    fdlibm `atan2` written with `+ - * /`, so it does not depend on libm or
    numpy's SIMD loops). The v1 value encodings (`int`, `dec`, `split`,
    `f64`) stay.
  - The encoder keeps, per channel, the smallest candidate (zstd -3 of each
    candidate's buffers), the value encoding included, and decodes every
    column back and compares bits (a mismatch is stored `f64`).
  - Numpy twins of every prediction (the decoder without the kernels).
- `native/src/telcols.rs` + `native/src/lib.rs`: `tif1_native.tel_integ`
  (the distance chain, in place), `tel_accx`, `tel_lateral`, `tel_spline`,
  `tel_interp` (interp prediction + residuals, in place), `tel_apply`
  (residuals, in place), GIL released, no new crate. Bit-identical to the
  numpy twins on all 1.3 M rows of the race and qualifying (and pinned by
  unit tests). `native/README.md` documents them.
- `src/tif1/core_utils/json_utils.py`: `NATIVE_TEL_*` handles and
  `native_columnar_decoder_available()`.
- `src/tif1/async_fetch.py`: the reader asks for `tel.v2` files, session
  marker `tel.v2.session`, and only when the native kernels are installed
  (the numpy decoder is ~6x slower per file: 25 vs 4.1 ms; without the
  kernels the waves keep the lap JSON, as with `telemetry_columnar=False`).
- `src/tif1/core_utils/constants.py`: `COLUMNAR_FORMAT_VERSION = 2`,
  `columnar_file_name`.
- `src/tif1/config.py`: comment of `telemetry_columnar` (default stays
  False until the files are published).
- `tools/data/encode_driver_telemetry.py`: writes v2; `--verify` decodes
  each file with the native kernels and with the numpy twins and compares
  both, and the pandas/polars frames, with the JSON.
- `design/TELEMETRY_COLUMNAR_FORMAT.md`: rewritten for v2 (every
  prediction's exact operation order, the spline, the dates, exactness).
  `AGENTS.md`: the columnar bullet.
- Tests (`tests/unit/test_columnar_telemetry.py`, 36): v2 file names, the
  candidate choice (a channel the prediction does not fit stays a value
  encoding), a synthetic fastf1-shaped lap stored with every predicted
  encoding and decoded exactly, the date recovery mod 512 ns, `lerp`
  without dates, spline edge cases, residual round trip of special bit
  patterns, the portable `atan2` against numpy, the numpy `integ` chain
  (runs, residual rows, NaN sums), numpy decode == native decode,
  native kernels == numpy predictions (skipped when the installed
  tif1-native predates them), the reader keeping the JSON without the
  kernels.
- Harnesses: `tools/perf/naxos_n7_cold_local.py` (copy of G3's local
  throttled CDN: each tree encodes its own files in a subprocess,
  `telemetry_columnar` on in both arms, `--candidate-python`),
  `tools/perf/naxos_n7_decode_bench.py` (decode time per session).

## 3. Measurements

### Bytes (2025 Bahrain; `tools/data/encode_driver_telemetry.py --cache ... --verify`)

| session | lap JSON, gzip | `tel.v1` | `tel.v2` | vs v1 | vs JSON |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Race (1128 laps, 119 files) | 71.32 MB | 52.50 MB | **10.79 MB** | −79.4% | −84.9% |
| Qualifying (277 laps, 34 files) | 24.27 MB | 17.79 MB | **4.51 MB** | −74.6% | −81.4% |

Steps (race): v1 value encodings 52.50 → + `lerp`/`integ`/`ratio`/`accx`
33.89 → + `lat` 22.84 → + `interp` (dates) 19.66 → + `spline`/`integs`
10.79 MB. What is left (race, per-column zstd estimate): `DistanceToDriverAhead`
at the car rows 2.5 MB (fastf1's driver-ahead distances depend on the other
cars), `time` 1.15 MB, x/y/z whole samples 1.35 MB and spline residuals
1.7 MB, `acc_*` residuals 1.8 MB, `rpm`/`speed`/`throttle` at the car rows
1.2 MB.

Exactness: 0 mismatching laps of 1405 (keys, dtypes, bits, DriverAhead codes
and categories), decoded with the native kernels and with the numpy twins;
the encoder's `--verify` also compares the pandas and polars frames
(`check_exact`) with the JSON's. Encoding time (level 19, with `--verify`,
box shared with other agents): race 39 s, qualifying 16 s.

## 4. Verdict: SHIP (format, kernels, encoder, reader), with a tif1-native release before the data publish

v2 is lossless (0 differences on 1405 laps with both decoders; identical
frame digests on both backends) and 79% smaller than v1 (85% smaller than
the gzipped JSON). On the throttled local CDN it beats v1 on every
telemetry workload, 8/8 pairs each, on both backends: all_tel −55%/−55%,
notebook4 −38%/−39%, driver_tel −22%/−24% (pandas/polars). On a 100 MB/s
link, where bytes are cheap, it still wins (all_tel −6.6% 7/8, notebook4
−13% 8/8, driver_tel −3.9% 7/8). Since v1 was never published, v2 simply
replaces it.

Order of release: tif1-native with the six kernels first (the reader asks
for v2 only when they are installed), then the data repositories publish
`tel.v2` files, then a tif1 release flips `telemetry_columnar` to True with
a `tif1-native` floor that has the kernels.

## 5. CHANGELOG

- Columnar telemetry files are now `{driver}/tel.v2.{first}.bin` (format 2,
  replacing the unpublished format 1). The channels fastf1 and the data
  pipeline compute from other channels are stored as the difference, in
  units in the last place, from a prediction made with plain IEEE
  arithmetic: the car channels interpolated at the position rows
  (`numpy.interp` over the laps' nanosecond dates), the position channels
  and `distance` interpolated with a quadratic spline, `distance`'s
  integration, `rel_distance`, and `acc_x/y/z`. Every decoded lap is still
  bit-identical to the lap JSON. The 2025 Bahrain race is 10.8 MB (format 1:
  52.5 MB, gzipped JSON: 71.3 MB), qualifying 4.5 MB. On a throttled local
  CDN (8 MB/s), `fetch_all_laps_telemetry()` takes 3.0 s instead of 8.2 s
  with format 1 (process 4.3 s instead of 9.5 s) and
  `laps.pick_driver("VER").telemetry` 215 ms instead of 478 ms; on a
  100 MB/s link `fetch_all_laps_telemetry()` is 2.3 s instead of 2.5 s. A
  file decodes in 4.1 ms instead of 2.3 ms. The predictions run in
  tif1-native (`tel_integ`, `tel_accx`, `tel_lateral`, `tel_spline`,
  `tel_interp`, `tel_apply`, GIL released); without them tif1 keeps reading
  the lap JSON. `tools/data/encode_driver_telemetry.py --verify` checks every
  lap with both the native and the numpy decoder.

## 6. Notes for the integrator

- **Release order.** The kernels are in `native/` (no new crate) but not on
  PyPI. The Cargo version is still 0.2.1: bump it (0.3.0) when releasing.
  `native.yml`'s CI job reruns the unit suite with the extension built from
  the commit, which runs the native-vs-numpy kernel test that is skipped in
  the main venv.
- **Data side.** Per session: `PYTHONPATH=src python
  tools/data/encode_driver_telemetry.py --json-dir "<repo>/<GP>/<Session>"
  --out-dir "<repo>/<GP>/<Session>" --verify` with tif1-native built from
  this tree. ~11 MB per race, ~4.5 MB per qualifying added to the `{year}`
  repository (+16% of the gzipped JSON). Encoding is ~40 s per race at
  level 19 including the verification (most of it the 512-candidate date
  search and the double decode).
- **Determinism is the contract.** Both decoders must compute the same
  predictions bit for bit: `+ - * /` and `fmod` only, in the documented
  order, no FMA (Rust never contracts; numpy's elementwise loops don't), no
  libm transcendental (the fdlibm `atan2` is spelled out), NaN predictions
  canonicalized to 0.0. Changing a prediction is a format version bump. One
  outside assumption: a process with flush-to-zero/denormals-are-zero turned
  on (some `-ffast-math` extensions do this process-wide) would compute
  other predictions when a prediction passes through a subnormal; telemetry
  values are not subnormal in practice.
- **Fit to upstream.** The predictions model fastf1's `get_telemetry()` and
  the TracingInsights acceleration code. If the pipeline changes (fastf1
  version, other formulas, sub-millisecond dates), the residuals grow and
  the encoder's size check falls back to the value encodings per channel:
  the files stay correct, only larger. Re-run the encoder's byte report on a
  new season before publishing.
- **Not reproduced exactly (yet).** x/y/z are exact on ~2/3 of the laps;
  the other third is a few ULPs to ~20 bits off (fastf1's spline includes
  the padding samples outside the lap and LAPACK's pivoted solve rounds
  differently); `acc_*` residuals come from OpenBLAS's fused dot products
  in `numpy.convolve` and libm's `atan2`. `DistanceToDriverAhead` at the car
  rows (2.5 MB of 10.8) needs the other drivers' data and stays raw.
- **Decode cost.** v2 costs ~200 ms more CPU per race than v1 (4.1 vs 2.3
  ms per file; the first version was 5.4 ms and tied v1 on all_tel over a
  100 MB/s link). What is left is mostly numpy orchestration around the
  kernels (unshuffle copies, masks, one call per column); a whole-file Rust
  decoder would cut most of it. On fast links that is where the remaining
  cold time is.
- **The numpy decoder** is correct but ~25 ms per file (the spline's Thomas
  solve runs in Python floats); the reader does not use it. Vectorizing the
  solve across the file's ~40 systems would make it ~10x faster if a
  pure-Python client ever needs the files.
- **Harness.** Both arms ran on `/tmp/naxos/venv-N7/bin/python` (the main
  venv's interpreter with tif1-native built from this tree, the main venv's
  packages through a `.pth`), so interpreter start-up is the same; the
  control tree does not call the new kernels.
- **Commits.** See `N7_COMMITS.md`.
- **Result files** (`.agents/perf-naxos/`): `N7_cold_local_{pandas,polars}_final.json`
  (final code), `N7_cold_local_{pandas,polars}.json` (first decoder),
  `N7_cold_local_pandas_round1.json` (intermediate format),
  `N7_cold_local_fast_pandas.json` (100 MB/s link, final decoder),
  `N7_cold_local_verify_{pandas,polars}.json` (digests),
  `N7_ab_pandas.json`, `N7_ab_pandas_rerun.json` (warm),
  `N7_parity_{pandas,polars}.json`.
- **pip was denied** in this sandbox, so pcodec (and ALP/Chimp
  implementations) were not measured; given that the predicted channels now
  cost ~2-4 bits per sample and the remaining raw floats are the anchor rows
  of `DistanceToDriverAhead`, a generic float codec could at most shave part
  of that 2.5 MB.
